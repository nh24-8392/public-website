Resources

Funding, collaborators, computing, code and data, and how the website was prepared

Funding, collaborators, computing resources, code and data of the project, with a statement on the use of AI tools in preparing the website.

This project

This website presents the results of the project A Computational Toolkit for Data-Driven Analysis and Modeling of Complex Spatiotemporal Dynamics Using Operator-Theoretic Techniques: Applications to Natural Hazards, supported by the Environmental Security Technology Certification Program (ESTCP) of the U.S. Department of War (DoW) under project NH24-8392 (Climate Resilience program, September 2024 to July 2027).

The project is led by Dimitrios Giannakis (principal investigator) and Joanna Slawinska (co-investigator), Department of Mathematics, Dartmouth College. The website is maintained by Joanna Slawinska.

Collaborators

The project is carried out in collaboration with the U.S. Air Force. The points of contact at the USAF 14th Weather Squadron are:

  • Louis Escamilla, Chief, Environmental Effects and Science Integration, Air Force Weather (A39W)
  • Benjamin J. Fulk, Lt Col, USAF
  • Jason Patla, Col, USAF

Computing resources

The analyses are run on panamint, a cluster of the Giannakis group in the Department of Mathematics at Dartmouth College, with eight NVIDIA H200 NVL GPUs. It has the capacity to analyze large, high-resolution datasets and to run the repeated delay-coordinate, kernel and synthetic-sampling experiments of the project.

Code

The methods are implemented in the open-source NLSA code, maintained by Dimitrios Giannakis in a public GitHub repository: github.com/dg227/NLSA. Its documentation is at dg227.github.io/NLSA.

Data

The ERA5 reanalysis data were provided by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts through the Copernicus Climate Change Service, and the ERSST data by the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration. The station records were provided by Air Force Weather and the 14th Weather Squadron.
