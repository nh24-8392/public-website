ERA5 monthly data

Monthly sea surface temperature for ENSO, the annual cycle and trends

How monthly ERA5 sea surface temperature is used in the demonstration: Koopman modes of the Indo-Pacific, ENSO forecasts based on the Niño 3.4 region, and a cross-check against ERSST.
WarningDraft for internal review

Not yet cleared for public release.

About monthly ERA5

ERA5 also comes as monthly averages of its hourly fields, available from the Copernicus Climate Data Store as ERA5 monthly averaged data on single levels, from 1940 to the present (Hersbach et al. 2020). Each new month is released a few days after the month ends.

Monthly data suit phenomena that evolve over seasons to decades. The demonstration uses monthly sea surface temperature (SST) to study and forecast ENSO, the dominant mode of climate variability on interannual timescales, which recurs every two to seven years. In ERA5, SST is taken from observational analyses rather than computed by the model.

How the demonstration uses monthly ERA5

Koopman modes of Indo-Pacific sea surface temperature

Setting Value
Variable Monthly sea surface temperature
Domain Indo-Pacific, 60°S–20°N, 28°E–70°W, on a 1° grid
Training period 1940–2019
Delay window 49 months
Method Koopman spectral analysis of the regularized generator

The annual cycle is kept in the input rather than removed beforehand. The spectrum of the Koopman operator then separates the annual cycle and its harmonics, an ENSO mode with an interannual period, and a long-term warming trend as distinct eigenfunctions, without any filtering of the data (Froyland et al. 2021, 2024). Projecting the SST field onto each eigenfunction gives the spatial pattern of that mode. The ENSO eigenfunction’s phase follows the stages of the ENSO cycle, and its amplitude follows how active ENSO is.

ENSO forecasts

Setting Value
Predictors SST over the Niño 3.4 region, on a 1° grid, in a delay window of 13 months
Target Niño 3.4 index: SST anomaly averaged over 5°S–5°N, 170°W–120°W (Trenberth 1997)
Method Kernel analog forecasting with 512 basis functions
Lead times 1 to 24 months
Anomalies Relative to the mean annual cycle; the long-term trend is not removed

Forecasts are updated every month, as soon as the new month of ERA5 data is released. Because the trend is not removed, part of a recent anomaly reflects long-term warming. This approach builds on earlier extended-range ENSO forecasts with operator-theoretic methods (Wang et al. 2020).

ENSO matters for all the demonstration regions. It is a principal source of winter precipitation variability in the southwestern United States (Cayan et al. 1999) and affects the frequency, location and intensity of typhoons in the western North Pacific (Wang and Chan 2002). Forecasts of the ENSO index therefore serve as conditioning information for the station forecasts.

Cross-check with ERSST

The Extended Reconstructed Sea Surface Temperature dataset, version 5 (Huang et al. 2017), from NOAA, extends back to 1854. It is used for analyses that need a longer record, such as trends over the industrial era, and as an independent check of ERA5 sea surface temperature.

Processing

  • Coarse grid. Fields are used on a 1° grid (every fourth point of ERA5’s 0.25° grid), which keeps the large-scale patterns that carry ENSO and trend signals while reducing the size of the problem.
  • Out-of-sample testing. Retrospective forecasts are trained only on earlier data and tested on later years. Training samples whose delay window or forecast window overlaps the test period are excluded.
  • Real-time forecasts. During the real-time season, the configuration is frozen before forecasts are issued, and models are retrained each month with the same settings as new data arrive.
